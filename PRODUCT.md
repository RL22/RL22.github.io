# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring managers for roles that own the web function — Web Platform Lead, Head of Web, Director of Web Platform — and the senior IC reqs that are the same job under an IC title (active applications: Vanta "Senior Web Developer", Hightouch "Web Engineer", PandaDoc "Senior Web Developer", DoorDash "Front-End Web Developer, B2B MarTech", Zep AI — founder-led outreach). One audience, not two: the lead-scope reader and the IC reader must both be served by the same page, because the site makes one claim and the resume points here. Most arrive from a resume or LinkedIn and spend 30–90 seconds scanning, deciding one thing: does this person own the marketing website as a product — strategy, system, and the code underneath — or build pages against someone else's plan? A founder reached by direct outreach arrives with no resume in hand, so the site has to carry that whole argument on its own. Secondary reader: a prospective Sprintz studio client evaluating craft.

## Product Purpose

Personal portfolio for Rodney L. Lewis (rl22.github.io). One positioning claim, stated everywhere: **I run the marketing website as a product.** Deliberately a single track — an audience-split architecture (separate engineering/strategy landing hubs) was evaluated and rejected: the case studies do not partition cleanly, and two narratives cost more credibility than the targeting gains. One conversion event: the reader books an intro call or submits the contact form. The site itself is the primary work sample; its craft, performance, and accessibility are proof of the claims it makes. Success: a screener leaves convinced Rodney operates at senior platform-owner level and reaches out.

## Brand Personality

Confident craftsman. Calm, precise, quietly senior. Warm and human (terracotta/cream, real photography) but engineering-first in voice: outcomes over adjectives, ownership verbs not management verbs. Never salesy, never shouty.

## Anti-references

- Generic dev-portfolio templates (Hero/About/Skills/Contact filled in with stock patterns).
- SaaS clichés: hero-metric blocks, identical icon-heading-text card grids, gradient text.
- Dark-mode-by-default developer/terminal aesthetic (neon on black).
- Marketing-agency hype tone; anything that reads "growth hacker" rather than "platform engineer".

## Design Principles

1. **The site is the work sample.** Every section must demonstrate the craft it claims: performance, accessibility, typographic care.
2. **Scan-first structure.** A screener gets the argument in 30 seconds; depth rewards the reader who stays.
3. **One claim, everywhere.** The claim is *I run the marketing website as a product*; no second positioning, no per-audience variant of the argument. The 30% Carrot figure is real but self-reported and unverifiable by a stranger, so it stays confined to one field (`carrot-cms-architecture`'s `outcome`, enforced by test) rather than repeated or led with. The metrics that lead are the ones a reader can check without trusting me: a Mednition page built in a five-month contract still live, unchanged, years later; Pendo's `/{family}/{use-case}/` URL architecture still running in production; Carrot's careers page still rendering live from the Greenhouse API. Durability a stranger can verify beats a percentage they have to take on faith.
4. **Warm restraint.** Committed terracotta accents on cream; personality through warmth and precision, not volume.
5. **Practice what you preach.** WCAG AA, reduced motion respected, real semantic structure: the portfolio of a platform owner behaves like a platform.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Contrast-checked text on cream/terracotta surfaces, `prefers-reduced-motion` honored for all reveal/float animations, semantic heading hierarchy, keyboard-reachable interactive elements.
