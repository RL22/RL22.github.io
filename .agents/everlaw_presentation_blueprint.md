# The Bar: Breaking Down Brent's Profile

Brent’s profile is a masterclass in positioning a technical web role as a strategic business driver. He doesn't just describe *what* he codes; he describes the *business impact* of his platform. 

Here is a breakdown of why this profile works so well, the key themes that Everlaw (and Chime) clearly value, and a blueprint for how you can present yourself to meet and exceed this bar.

## 1. The Core Narrative: The Website as a "Growth Product"
Brent positions the marketing website not as a digital brochure, but as an **end-to-end growth product**.
*   **The phrasing:** "building scalable, high-performance web platforms that drive acquisition and growth."
*   **The implication:** He isn't just taking tickets from marketing; he is actively driving pipeline and demand generation.

## 2. The Intersection of Four Domains
He explicitly places himself at the center of the organization:
*   **Marketing:** Understanding acquisition, conversion, and brand.
*   **Product:** Treating the web platform with product management rigor (roadmaps, KPIs).
*   **Engineering:** Architecture, infrastructure, CI/CD, and tech stack.
*   **Analytics:** Measurement frameworks, dashboards, and data-driven decisions.

## 3. The Tech Stack & Architecture Focus
He proves his technical chops by highlighting a modern, composable stack, but always ties it back to a business benefit:
*   **The Stack:** Contentful (Headless CMS), 11ty (SSG), Netlify, GitHub, Datadog, Percy.
*   **The Business Value:** "modular, component-based architecture enabling scalable content creation and rapid iteration and content velocity." (Translation: *I make it so marketing can build pages faster without needing developers for every small change.*)

## 4. Systems of Optimization (CRO & SEO)
It's not just about building it; it's about optimizing it.
*   He highlights **CRO (Conversion Rate Optimization)** and **Experimentation**.
*   He mentions "hypothesis-driven testing," "personalization," and driving a "20% YoY increase in testing velocity."
*   He owns site health across performance, technical SEO, and accessibility.

## 5. Operational Leadership
He shows he can run a high-functioning team.
*   He defines operating models, sprint planning, prioritization, and QA/release workflows.
*   He mentions mentoring and building career development goals for his team.

# Your Blueprint: How to Present Yourself

To hit this bar, your resume, cover letter, and interview talk-track need to adopt this exact framing. 

### Step 1: Elevate Your Pitch
Stop talking about building web pages. Start talking about building **web engines for pipeline generation**. Use words like *velocity, scale, modularity, acquisition, and performance.*

### Step 2: Highlight the "Component-Driven" Strategy
Everlaw clearly values a decoupled, headless approach (Contentful/Netlify). If you have experience building component libraries or design systems that empower marketing teams to self-serve content, make that the centerpiece of your technical narrative.

### Step 3: Showcase the Data Loop
Don't just say you "built" things. Explain how you measured them. Talk about A/B testing, integrating analytics, and using data to inform the web roadmap. Show that you understand the funnel.

### Step 4: The Cross-Functional Bridge
In interviews (especially with the contacts we identified earlier like the CMO and Demand Gen leaders), emphasize that you speak the language of marketing as fluently as the language of engineering. You are the translation layer between business goals and technical execution.

### The Missing Piece (How to exceed his bar):
Brent mentions he is *currently* focused on AI and personalized high-speed growth platforms. If you have experience or a strong vision for how to integrate AI into the web workflow (e.g., AI-driven personalization, automated A/B testing generation, AI in the CMS), **lead with that**. It shows you are already skating to where the puck is going.
